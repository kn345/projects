Use Case 1

Rachel establishes her academic identity

An emerging PhD candidate builds a discoverable, self-owned research presence — independent of her advisor — over the course of the academic year.

Before the platform
"The only reason anyone takes my emails seriously is because my advisor is cc'd."
  • Profile page maintained by the department — she can't update it
  • No way to signal she's open to collaborating with junior researchers
  • Cold outreach almost never gets a reply
  • Industry work history treated as a credibility gap, not an asset
  • Updating Google Scholar, ResearchGate, and her lab page separately
With the platform
"I have one profile I control that tells the full story of my research — on my own terms."
  • Canonical, researcher-controlled profile she can update anytime
  • Explicit "open to collaboration" signal — visible to the right people
  • Contextualized inbound inquiries she can evaluate with full information
  • Industry work reframed as applied research context
  • One link to share — live, timestamped research output
September

Set up her research identity

Rachel is assigned her thesis topic and begins scoping her research inquiry. She creates her profile for the first time.

Outlines thesis focus & methodology Lists thesis advisors & their domains Flags target conferences (CHI, CSCW) Sets "open to junior collaborations"
What changed: She is now discoverable and self-described — independent of her advisor's name.
October

Build her academic year plan

Rachel maps out submission deadlines, identifies researchers in adjacent areas, and refines her collaboration settings.

Marks conference submission deadlines Discovers peers with overlapping interests Sets collaboration type preferences
What changed: The platform surfaces early-career researchers who are also open to collaborating — no cold outreach required.
February

Submit a paper — and get seen

Rachel submits a workshop paper and links it to her profile as "in review." Her profile now shows live, timestamped research output.

Links paper (status: in review) Shares one profile link in outreach
When Marcus searches her area this month, Rachel's profile surfaces — with current work, stated interests, and a clear collaboration signal.
May

Conference season — meet the right people

Rachel marks herself as attending CHI. The platform surfaces other attendees with overlapping research interests before the event.

Marks conference attendance Gets matched with overlapping attendees Initiates pre-conference meeting request Industry history shown as "applied research context"
What changed: Networking is intentional, not accidental. She arrives at CHI with meetings already scheduled.
Use Case 2

Marcus finds the right academic partner

An industry research lead stops searching blindly and builds a credible, long-term university relationship — starting with finding Rachel.

Before the platform
"We have the resources to fund research, but finding the right person takes too long."
  • Searching Google Scholar and lab pages written for academic audiences
  • No way to search researchers by actual focus area
  • Unclear whether to email the professor, the department, or partnerships office
  • Weeks go by without a response — no indication of availability
  • Academics may view industry funding as suspicious
With the platform
"I can see exactly who is working on what, whether they're open to industry collaboration, and how to reach them."
  • Search by research focus area, not just institution or name
  • Filter for researchers open to industry partnerships
  • Structured inquiry channel — no ambiguity about who to contact
  • Researcher sees full company context before deciding to reply
  • Existing partnerships visible on his profile — builds credibility upfront
September

Marcus has a research need

Marcus's team is scoping a project in HCI and recommendation systems. He needs an academic collaborator with a specific expertise profile.

Searches by research focus area Filters for "open to industry" signal Rachel's profile surfaces
What's different: Marcus doesn't have to guess. He can see immediately that Rachel is open to working with industry partners.
October

Marcus initiates contact — the right way

Marcus sends a collaboration inquiry through the platform. Rachel receives it with full context about who he is and what he's looking for.

Sends structured inquiry (topic, engagement type) Links company's existing academic partnerships Rachel sees full context — not a cold email
Rachel responds. The platform's credibility signals reduced the friction on both sides.
November

Partnership is formalized

Rachel and Marcus agree to a scoped collaboration. Both update their profiles to reflect the new relationship.

Partnership linked on both profiles Rachel's industry connection shown as credibility Marcus's team has documented academic relationship
The collaboration is visible to both teams — reducing internal skepticism about the engagement.
Ongoing

Relationship grows over time

The platform maintains a record of the collaboration as it evolves — publications, co-appearances, and renewal prompts.

Shared publications linked to both profiles Co-conference appearances tracked Renewal prompt when engagement period ends
What changed: A one-off search became a long-term, documented university partnership.
